Industrial IoT & time-series foundation models

ChengShi Tech Ltd., Xi'an · Co-founder & Lead AI Scientist · 2023–2025

Co-founded the company and built its AI-driven industrial IoT platform from 0 to 1, leading a 25-person team (3 AI researchers) to deliver production systems serving 200,000+ residential users and 2,000+ enterprise clients across multiple regions. Scaled to commercial partnerships and coordinated with Series A investors (a publicly-listed Chinese energy company) before transitioning to an advisory role.

  • Time-series foundation model with adaptive multi-scale decomposition
    Models minute-to-monthly predictions simultaneously through a Lipschitz-constrained cross-temporal-scale architecture, with a Mixture-of-Experts framework whose experts specialise by modality and regional consumption pattern, plus transfer learning from open-domain time-series datasets.
  • Heterogeneous graph neural network
    Multi-level hierarchy (user → district/enterprise → city) integrating alternative data: weather, building age, upstream industrial signals (coal, metal smelting), pipeline usage history.
  • Gas leak early-warning system (40%+ → 17% false alarms)
    Replaced an expert-plus-statistical baseline with deep-learning detection; deployed in production with dual-track human–AI verification.
  • Demand forecasting under asymmetric loss
    Under-purchasing is 3–5× more costly than over-purchasing under China's energy quota system. Preliminary results show 5–10% annual cost savings for regional gas companies; scheduled for production deployment with a 3-year validation protocol.
  • Multimodal LLM-agent for daily maintenance
    Accesses 11 independent local databases with knowledge-graph-enhanced RAG to analyse, auto-execute, visualise and explain status and key trends for 120,000 local users. Running on the collaborator's platform.
  • Data infrastructure
    3 years of history from 200,000+ smart meters at minute-level granularity; cold-start strategies (regional-level priors, pre-trained fine-tuning) and model lifecycle management with versioned checkpoints.

Early LLM product ventures

Co-founder / principal engineer · part-time · 2021–2023

Two ventures run alongside research, both among the earliest production deployments of LLM systems in their verticals. Company names are withheld here.

  • Multi-agent e-commerce content pipeline
    Led a 7-person team to build an end-to-end pipeline from market-intelligence extraction to automated product listing. Hybrid LLM orchestration — GPT-4 for planning and decision-making, Qwen-LoRA for cost-efficient local generation — balancing capability against inference cost. Fine-tuned Stable Diffusion for generative product design (tattoo patterns, T-shirt graphics) and integrated Midjourney and Kling APIs for social-media video. A human-in-the-loop design allowed semi-daily review cycles while the system operated autonomously in between. Exit: the technology stack (codebase and documentation) was acquired by an established mid-sized e-commerce company.
  • AI-assisted report generation for a consulting practice
    An ESG report generation system serving public-company compliance and competitive-intelligence needs. Achieved a 10× efficiency improvement — initial draft time fell from 2–3 weeks with a 5-person team to 2 days — through fine-tuned language models, managing the technical evolution from fine-tuned BERT to GPT-3.5 against a generation-quality/training-cost trade-off. Built the annotation pipeline over 2 years of historical reports, mapping ESG standards to report logic and turning PDF documents into structured training data. Delivered as an internal tool still in use.

Learned image compression

Deep Render Ltd. & Imperial College London (Innovate UK) · Research Scientist / Research Associate · 2020–2021

Supervised by Prof. William Knottenbelt. Developed a learned (generative) image compression system that circumvented JPEG-2000 and WebP patent barriers while achieving 30%+ compression improvement. Independently contributed a neural architecture search framework for efficient operators and a differentiable quantisation bottleneck design (internal use in product line V0.1.3).

  • Three gaps for quantisation in learned image compression
    S. Pan, C. Finlay, C. Besenbruch, W. Knottenbelt
    IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition Workshops (CVPRW), 720–726 · 2021

University of Kent · Ph.D. Electrical Engineering · 2019
