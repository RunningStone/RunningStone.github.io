Humans are the unrepeatable samplers of a non-ergodic world, and the diverse observations they supply are irreplaceable. Simulation offers an ensemble seen from an information-limited viewpoint, and keeps optimising the policy. Intelligent machines contribute reliable execution. Social communities shape the optimisation objectives and the reward loops.

My long-term research interest is in long-horizon, self-improving agents that can take over repetitive work currently performed by humans. To get there I build the complete evaluation-to-training loop — controlled environments, reproducible rollouts, evaluators that turn observed behaviour into training signals, and reward-driven post-training. Alongside this systems work I study two foundations of reliable long-horizon agency: how task structure limits multi-agent success, and how lifelong learning can be formalised around the forgetting–plasticity trade-off under survival pressure.

The same instinct runs through everything earlier: understand the first principles of the architecture, engage deeply with the data, and carry the result all the way to something that measurably works — breaking patent barriers in learned image compression, replacing £7,000+ spatial transcriptomics assays with routine histology, deploying industrial IoT serving 200,000+ users. I care less about novelty in isolation than about the distance a result travels before it stops being a demo.
