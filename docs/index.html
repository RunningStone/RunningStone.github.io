Humans are the unrepeatable samplers of a non-ergodic world, and the diverse observations they supply are irreplaceable. Simulation offers an ensemble seen from an information-limited viewpoint, and keeps optimising the policy. Intelligent machines contribute reliable execution. Social communities shape the optimisation objectives and the reward loops.

I study agents that can work over long periods and improve with experience, with the aim of taking on more of the repetitive work people currently do. Much of my work connects evaluation to training: controlled environments, reproducible rollouts, evaluators that turn behaviour into training signals, and reward-based post-training. I also study how task structure limits multi-agent success, and how to formalise lifelong learning around the forgetting–plasticity trade-off under survival pressure.

Earlier, I worked on learned image compression, computational biology and industrial IoT: navigating compression patent restrictions, predicting from routine histology information that would otherwise require £7,000+ spatial transcriptomics assays, and deploying systems for 200,000+ users. These projects taught me to work closely with both the model and the data, then follow through on the constraints of actual use. I care about what a result can solve and whether it keeps working beyond the demo.
