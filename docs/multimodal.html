Multimodal & Virtual Cell

I work on using models to reduce the need for expensive experiments. This began with predicting spatial gene expression from routine H&E slides, reducing reliance on £7,000+ spatial transcriptomics assays. I then moved to modelling how cell states change over time, with the aim of generating and designing cell trajectories.

This work is based in the Secrier Lab, Computational Cancer Biology at the UCL Genetics Institute. The lab studies somatic evolution in cancer: mutation, cellular plasticity and stress response, and the co-evolution of cells and their microenvironment during therapy resistance. Its tumour-dormancy research is supported by a UKRI Future Leaders Fellowship. As the group's sole AI expert, I was responsible for the modelling work, AI infrastructure, tool development and server management.

1 · Pathology vision–language models → spatial transcriptomics

I trained and fine-tuned multimodal foundation models on gigapixel histopathology images to predict gene expression from routine H&E slides. The inferred spatial cell-population structure agreed with spatial transcriptomics (ST) ground truth, allowing studies that had been constrained by the £7,000+ cost of ST assays.

  • PEKA — parameter-efficient knowledge transfer
    Accepted at MICCAI 2025, in the top 9%.
    Paper · MICCAICode
  • Few-shot adaptation of general vision models
    Following the MICCAI work, I adapted a general vision foundation model with a small amount of pathology data. It matches or exceeds pathology-specific models such as UNI on gene-expression prediction from H&E. The revised manuscript is under review.

2 · Single-cell foundation models & cellular dynamics

This work models how cells move between states. I use generative models to describe those transitions, with the aim of guiding cell-state and trajectory design.

  • ODE / flow-matching single-cell foundation model
    Uses ordinary differential equations and flow matching to model temporal cell-state transitions and causal disentanglement, supporting trajectory design such as inducing a specific differentiation path from an initial state. Wet-lab validation with collaborators is underway.
  • Generative dormancy trajectories (scFM + Schrödinger bridge)
    Combines a single-cell foundation model with a Schrödinger bridge to generate the full trajectory from a non-dormant tumour cell into dormancy. Validation experiments are underway.
  • Evolution-aware cell prototypes
    Adds supervision from time and cell-evolution order to single-cell foundation models through a learned dictionary of cell prototypes. The prototypes are tied to evolutionary trajectories, addressing a weakness of scFMs relative to classical bioinformatics methods in identifying new cell populations. The revised manuscript is under review.

3 · Transcriptomic language modelling

I represent transcriptomic data as sequences that a model can read, then fine-tune it to find biomarkers that conventional differential-expression analysis may miss.

  • EMT state classification with LLMs
    Fine-tuned large language models on transcriptomic data associated with epithelial–mesenchymal transition and tumour dormancy, identifying new biomarkers and therapeutic targets. The revised manuscript is under review at Nature Communications.
    Paper · bioRxiv
  • Foundation-model alignment with dynamical systems
    Combines multimodal foundation models with ODE/SDE models of cell dynamics to study the tumour microenvironment and dormancy. This collaborative study is under review at Nature Cancer.
    Paper · bioRxiv

4 · Agents for bioinformatics

I also build agents that carry out bioinformatics analyses. This was my first practical use of reinforcement-learning post-training.

  • RL-enhanced agent for cell-type classification
    An agent that retrieves dormancy markers from the literature and runs single-cell validation workflows. I proposed a benchmark for it and used reinforcement-learning post-training to improve its performance.
