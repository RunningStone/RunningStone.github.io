I am an AI researcher who builds end-to-end systems that solve previously intractable real-world problems. My approach rests on two principles: understanding the first principles of AI architectures, and engaging deeply with the characteristics of domain-specific data.

My long-term interest is in **long-horizon, self-improving agents** that can take over repetitive work currently performed by humans. To get there I build the complete **evaluation-to-training loop**: controlled environments, reproducible rollouts, evaluators that turn observed behaviour into training signals, and reward-driven post-training. Alongside this systems work I study two foundations of reliable long-horizon agency — how task structure limits multi-agent success, and how lifelong learning can be formalised around the forgetting–plasticity trade-off under survival pressure.

The same instinct runs through my earlier work: breaking patent barriers in learned image compression, making £7,000+ spatial transcriptomics assays accessible through vision models, and deploying industrial IoT systems serving 200,000+ users. I care less about technical novelty in isolation than about translating it into measurable real-world impact.
